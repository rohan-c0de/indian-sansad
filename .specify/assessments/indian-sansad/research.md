## What sansad.in and the eLibrary Publish, and How Each Is Accessible

### Verified first-hand (this session)

- **An unauthenticated JSON API exists.** `GET https://sansad.in/api_ls/member` returned JSON with `"metaDatasDto":{"currentPageNumber":1,"perPageSize":5426,"totalElements":5426,"totalPages":1}` — the full historical + sitting Lok Sabha roster in one response, no API key, no auth challenge. Per-record fields observed: `mpsno, initial, firstName, lastName, mpLastFirstName, mpFirstLastName, gender, partyFname, partySname, stateName, constName, profession, presentFaddr, presentLaddr, delhiPhone, permanentFaddr, permanentLaddr, personalPhone, lastLoksabha, lsExpr, age, email, phone, noOfTerms, status, maritalStatus, imageUrl, profileUrl, dob, numberOfSons, numberOfDaughters, qualification, freedom, profession2, categoryCode, createdAt, updatedAt`. — [source: sansad.in/api_ls/member, fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: **high**, cited)
- **The API is actively maintained.** The sampled record carried `"createdAt":"2025-08-15 ...","updatedAt":"2026-10-05 ..."` — updated 3 days before this check. — [source: same] (confidence: high, cited)
- **`api_rs/member` is NOT the Rajya Sabha equivalent.** `GET https://sansad.in/api_rs/member` → **HTTP 404**. The RS member route differs and was not identified. — [source: fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: high, cited)
- **Data-driven HTML pages are empty shells; scraping the HTML yields nothing.** `https://sansad.in/` returned only a title and a "Skip intro" link. `https://sansad.in/ls/legislation/bills` returned the table scaffolding (`Bill Number`, `Short Title`, `Ministry`, `Member`, `Date of Introduction`) plus `![no data available](./images/no-data.png)` and a result count of `0`. Content is fetched client-side. — [source: fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: **high**, cited)
- **Static explainer pages *are* server-rendered.** `https://sansad.in/ls/debates/introduction` and `https://sansad.in/ls/knowledge-centre/collections` returned real prose and link lists. The split is: narrative pages render, record listings do not. — [source: fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: high, cited)
- **Committee routes exist but need parameters.** `api_ls/committee/lsRSAllReports` → **HTTP 500**; `api_rs/committee/committee-reports` → **HTTP 400**. Neither 404'd, so the routes resolve; required query parameters were not determined. `api_ls/legislation/bills` → **404** (wrong path). — [source: fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: high for the status codes, cited; **low** for what the correct parameters are)
- **No sitemap.** `https://sansad.in/sitemap.xml` → **HTTP 404**. There is no crawlable index of the URL space. — [source: fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: high, cited)
- **Debate records exist in six distinct forms, with different release timings.** Quoted from sansad.in: *Original Version* "contains proceedings in English and Hindi as they actually take place in the House. It also contains English/Hindi translation of speeches made in regional languages."; *Hindi Version*; *English Version* "contains Lok Sabha proceedings in English and English translation of the proceedings which take place in Hindi or in any regional language."; *Uncorrected Debates* "released onto the Homepage on the next day for the use of the members, media persons, researchers and the public at large."; *Final Edited Debates* "released under the icon Finally Edited Debated onto the Homepage as and when got vetted by the members. The release of edited debate generally take 10-15 days."; *Synopsis of Debate* — "a gist of important suggestions and points made during the Debates", explicitly "not an authoritative record." — [source: sansad.in/ls/debates/introduction] (confidence: **high**, cited)
- **Parliament Digital Library collection list** (verbatim, from sansad.in/ls/knowledge-centre/collections): "Part 1 (Questions And Answers)", "Part 2 (Other than Questions And Answers)", "Prime Minister Speeches", "Lok Sabha Debates (Text)", "Lok Sabha Debates (Hindi)", "Budget Speeches", "Lok Sabha Bulletin I", "Parliamentary Committee Reports", "Presidential Address", "Resume of Work Done by Lok Sabha", Books, "Information Bulletin", Periodicals, and historical series "Constituent Assembly-Draft Making", "Constituent Assembly-Legislative", "Council of States", "Indian Legislative Council", "Legislative Assembly", "Provisional Parliament". — [source: fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: high, cited)
- **The eLibrary is two separate corpora.** `elibrary.sansad.in` describes (1) *Digital Library* — "more than one lakh digitized documents comprising approx. 1 crore pages", covering "Papers Laid on the Table of House, Reports, Books, Acts, Bills, and Periodicals", of which "89,000 documents are available"; and (2) *Digital Business Document (E-Parlib)* — "more than 12 lakh documents having approx. 50 lakh digitized pages", covering debates 1854–1952 and 1st–18th Lok Sabha, committee reports, presidential addresses, PM speeches, budget speeches. It is built on "DSpace technology" with migration to "version 7.4". — [source: elibrary.sansad.in, fetched 2026-10-08] (confidence: medium — these are the site's own self-reported figures, cited)

## Users & Demand

- **An authoritative third-party assessment says the records are hard to find and hard to use** — quoted verbatim: "their site architecture, their abysmal search functionalities, and the less-than-useful format of the records on the sites make it clear that the records are both hard to find, and hard to make use of by researchers and citizens." This directly supports the owner's premise, from an independent source. — [source: newamerica.org, Pranesh Prakash working paper] (confidence: **high** as a cited expert judgement; **medium** as evidence of user demand — it is an expert's stated view, not observed user behaviour)
- **Bulk access has been asked for officially and refused**: "the data custodians (as listed on data.gov.in) for both the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha explained that whatever was on the website was all that was accessible." — [source: same] (confidence: high, cited)
- **Observed behaviour, not just stated want — researchers are doing the conversion work themselves, one corpus at a time.** A Zenodo dataset of Lok Sabha Committee on Subordinate Legislation reports (1954–2023) exists precisely because the originals were "scanned pdfs" that had to be "collated and converted into machine readable formats for analysis" (7.3 MB of `.txt`, 35 downloads). — [source: zenodo.org/records/17198275] (confidence: **high** as revealed preference; the 35 downloads indicate a *small* audience)
- **A second researcher-built dataset** covers LS + RS questions, debates and special mentions for all sitting MPs as of 31.12.2025, scraped "from https://sansad.in/poi/", 7 `.xlsx` files, 31.4 MB, CC-BY-4.0, with **549 downloads and 873 views** since release on 2026-01-04. This is the clearest quantified demand signal found. — [source: zenodo.org/records/18146342] (confidence: **high** for the figures, cited; note they measure demand for *a static dump*, not for a website)
- **At least 8 independent GitHub projects** are building on this data (SansadSaar, indiavotes/proceedings-data, parliamentwatch-data + 2 forks, commoner-probe, lok-rajya-sabha-qa, loksabha-questions, eparlibscrapR, lok-sabha-debates-analysis, Indian_Parliament_QnA). Developer interest is demonstrated, repeatedly, by behaviour. — [source: github.com, WebSearch] (confidence: high, cited)
- **A sitting MP is publicly asking for exactly one of the unmet needs below.** Derek O'Brien (TMC floor leader, Rajya Sabha) published "This Parliament Session, Watch What Is 'Expunged'" (2026-03-09), warning of "a worrying trend where any word, phrase, or even entire paragraphs, critical of the ruling dispensation, is deemed unparliamentary and is expunged". — [source: derekobrien.in] (confidence: high that it was said, cited; it is an interested party's claim, not an audited finding)
- **Academic demand is established**: the TCPD-IPD dataset (LS Question Hour, 1999–2019) underpins a published paper using LDA/LDAseq topic modelling, whose authors note "it is difficult to analyze such a huge collection manually". — [source: github.com/AdhyaSuman/Indian_Parliament_QnA] (confidence: medium, cited)

Every demand signal located traces to a researcher, a developer, a think tank or a politician. The citizen audience in the intake is **unevidenced** so far. ## Market & Context

- **What people cope with today**: PRS summaries for analysis; one-off Zenodo dumps for research; GitHub mirrors for developers; manual PDF reading for everyone else. No single continuously-updated public index of primary parliamentary documents was found. — [synthesis of the above] (confidence: medium)
- **The structural reason the data is awkward is a missing digital workflow, not neglect of a website.** The recommendation in the literature is to adopt Akoma Ntoso — "a structured and semantically marked-up format for recordkeeping and to establish a digital workflow for both houses of Parliament". Until that happens, PDFs remain the authoritative artefact and any third-party product is doing format repair. — [source: newamerica.org] (confidence: high, cited)
- **Cost of doing nothing**: the material stays usable only by those who can write a scraper and run OCR. Observed proxy — the only machine-readable committee-report corpus found (1954–2023) had **35 downloads**, while a ready-made spreadsheet dump of current-MP activity had **549**. Convenience appears to drive use by an order of magnitude. — [source: zenodo.org, both records] (confidence: medium; two data points, different subject matter — suggestive, not established)
- **Link rot is actively destroying access.** Two formerly canonical hosts (`eparlib.nic.in`, `loksabha.nic.in`) no longer resolve, yet they are still cited in published datasets and library guides. Citations to Indian parliamentary documents are breaking now. — [source: fetches 2026-10-08 + eparlibscrapR README still targeting eparlib.nic.in] (confidence: **high** for the DNS failures; medium for the breadth of consequence)

## Capability Gaps & Candidate Uses (evidence-led, not recommendations)

Recorded because the owner's direction asks for uses not possible today and `intake.md` requests a shortlist with evidence. **No option is endorsed, sized or designed here.**

1. **Answer text at scale — the central gap.** Questions are available as metadata; *answers* are machine-readable nowhere. RS: "answer text is `null` for every record". LS: answers in "randomly-suffixed PDFs", and the one author who got closest said "OCR'ing ~34,700 PDFs was out of scope." Everything downstream — ministry evasion detection, statistics buried in answer tables, contradictions between answers over time — is blocked on this. — [source: github.com/herrrickshaw/lok-rajya-sabha-qa] (gap confidence: **high**)
2. **Uncorrected-vs-final-edited debate diffing.** Verified: uncorrected text is published "on the next day" for "the public at large"; final edited appears "as and when got vetted by the members", typically 10–15 days later. The record therefore *changes*, and only someone capturing both versions can see the delta. No prior art found doing this. A sitting MP is publicly asking for it to be watched. **Carries a serious risk — see Evidence Against #1.** (gap confidence: **high**; viability: contested) Owner note: not of interest to the owner. Not a prohibition; simply not to be pursued.
3. **A permanent citation/resolver layer for parliamentary documents.** Grounded in verified DNS death of two canonical hosts while third parties still link to them. (gap confidence: medium-high)
4. **A Hindi–English aligned parliamentary corpus.** Verified: Parliament produces Hindi and English *verbatim translations of the same proceedings*, plus translations of regional-language speeches. The major Hindi–English parallel corpora found (IIT Bombay, 1.49M segments; HindEnCorp, 274k; Mann ki Baat) do **not** include parliamentary proceedings. A public-domain, domain-specific, continuously-growing aligned corpus appears unexploited. — [source: sansad.in debates page + WebSearch of MT corpora] (gap confidence: **medium** — absence rests on one literature search, not a systematic review)
5. **Committee recommendation → Action Taken Report chaining.** Recommendations "are not legally binding"; reports "are rarely discussed on the House floor"; PAC alone makes ~180 recommendations a year with ~80% claimed accepted. Partially attempted (commoner-probe lists action-taken responses as a subtype). (gap confidence: low–medium; snippet-sourced)
6. **"Papers Laid on the Table" as an unindexed corpus.** Named in the elibrary's 1-lakh-document Digital Library segment; no prior art found indexing it. Agency annual reports and audit material arrive here. (gap confidence: low — nobody-does-it is unproven)
7. **A per-MP activity record that does not pretend to be a voting record.** Verified: "individual legislators' votes are not usually recorded"; "Less than 2 percent of the bills considered by the Lok Sabha are decided by counted votes"; 16th LS had "90 divisions ... But only 11 bills were voted upon." PRS already publishes MP track records, so this is the most crowded option. (gap confidence: low)

### Additional candidate uses (ideation pass, 2026-10-08)

Eight further candidates, none overlapping the seven above and none involving uncorrected-vs-final debate comparison. **Endorsed: none. Designed: none.** Per the owner's instruction, **no money or infrastructure-cost limit was applied to this pass** (lifted for ideation only); the ~2h/week upkeep ceiling was applied throughout. One current-term English debates PDF was observed to have a text layer (owner test); Hindi, other volumes and written answers are untested. **A8.1 — Ministry-side answering-load and question-mix profile.** *Audience*: journalists, researchers. *New capability*: see which ministries absorb the most questions, the starred/unstarred mix per ministry, and how that shifts session to session — today the metadata exists but sits behind a client-side interface with no bulk route, so nobody publishes the ministry-side view. *Evidence*: question metadata carries "subject, ministry, member names, type and date" for 34,720 LS questions [cited — the source does not name the term those cover]; PRS publishes "vital statistics" but apparently no API or bulk download [cited, unconfirmed]; "abysmal search functionalities" [cited]. *OCR*: no — metadata only. *Personal-data endpoint*: no. *Cost*: expected within free tiers; comparable projects run on GitHub Actions + Pages/Cloudflare Pages [cited].

**A8.2 — "This has been asked before": recurring-question detector across sessions and both Houses.** *Audience*: journalists, citizens. *New capability*: on seeing a question, see the prior near-identical ones and their dates, ministries and answer documents — no prior art in this file links repeat questions to each other. *Evidence*: RS "returns full question text" [cited]; LS listing "only exposes the subject line" [cited]; ~243K–280K LS question records already extracted by others [cited]. *OCR*: no for the matching itself; the linked answers stay PDFs. *Personal-data endpoint*: no. *Cost*: text matching on subject lines is cheap; **semantic similarity over ~280K records could exceed a free tier** [ASSUMPTION — not measured].

**A8.3 — Continuously-updated plain-language weekly digest, English and Hindi.** *Audience*: citizens (the owner's student/civics learner and curious general reader). *New capability*: a short recurring summary of what the Houses did, in both languages — today the nearest official artefact is the per-sitting "Synopsis of Debate", which the Secretariat itself calls "not an authoritative record", published as PDFs. *Evidence*: synopsis and uncorrected/final-edited release timings [cited, verified]; Hindi and English versions are both official [cited]. *OCR*: unknown for current-term PDFs (see preamble); plausibly avoidable by working from question and bill metadata instead [ASSUMPTION]. *Personal-data endpoint*: no. *Cost*: **LLM summarisation per sitting day would likely exceed free tiers**; Parliament sits on the order of tens of days per session [ASSUMPTION — neither the sitting count nor the token volume was measured here].

**A8.4 — Bill stage tracking with alerts by subject or ministry.** *Audience*: citizens, journalists, researchers. *New capability*: be notified when a bill a person cares about changes stage, rather than checking a page. *Evidence*: bills carry "every bill with its introduction date, stage dates, and status" [cited]; the bills HTML page renders as a shell with `0` results [verified]. **Overlap to note honestly**: PRS already does bill tracking [cited], so only the alerting and the machine-readable stage history would be new, and PRS's exact coverage is unconfirmed. *OCR*: no. *Personal-data endpoint*: no. *Cost*: within free tiers [ASSUMPTION]; email or push delivery at scale is the only plausible overrun [ASSUMPTION].

**A8.5 — Constituency and state lens: what the MPs for one area actually raise.** *Audience*: citizens (the owner's "voter checking their MP"), journalists. *New capability*: look up a district or state and see the subjects its MPs have raised, instead of starting from a member's name. *Evidence*: the member endpoint returns `stateName` and `constName` per MP [verified]; questions carry asking-member names [cited]; MyNeta covers affidavits and explicitly "does not cover" parliamentary activity [cited]. *OCR*: no. *Personal-data endpoint*: **yes — but only its non-personal fields** (name, party, state, constituency); requires the field allowlist flagged in Data & Constraints, and must exclude phone, personal email, address and family composition. *Cost*: within free tiers [ASSUMPTION].

**A8.6 — Parliament-as-institution cohort dataset from the member record.** *Audience*: researchers, journalists. *New capability*: analyse the composition of the House over time — age, gender, profession, qualification, number of terms, party and state — from the Secretariat's own biographical fields, which no project in this file publishes as an analysable cohort. Distinct from MyNeta, which draws on ECI affidavits (assets, criminal cases, education, income), not these fields [cited]. *Evidence*: one unauthenticated response returned all `"totalElements":5426` records with those fields, historical and sitting [verified]. *OCR*: no. *Personal-data endpoint*: **yes — this is the personal-data endpoint**; the whole use rests on it, so the allowlist is load-bearing rather than incidental, and the DPDP framing in Data & Constraints is an untested ASSUMPTION. Fields that must be excluded: `personalPhone`, `delhiPhone`, `email`, `presentFaddr`/`presentLaddr`, `permanentFaddr`/`permanentLaddr`, `dob`, `maritalStatus`, `numberOfSons` and `numberOfDaughters`. Note that `dob` is excluded while `age` is retained, even though one derives from the other — this file does not settle that. *Cost*: trivial — the entire roster arrived in a single JSON response [verified].

**A8.7 — Upstream change-and-breakage feed.** *Audience*: researchers, journalists, and anyone citing these documents. *New capability*: a public record of what appeared, what vanished and which URLs stopped resolving — nobody in this file publishes upstream change observability, and the owner's own stated preference is to be flagged when upstream shifts. *Evidence*: three hosts failed during this assessment (`eparlib.nic.in`, `loksabha.nic.in` DNS; `pprloksabha.sansad.in` DNS) [verified]; no sitemap exists (`sitemap.xml` → 404) [verified]; the API has "no contract, no versioning and no deprecation notice" [cited]; one prior archival project returned 404 [verified]. **Adjacency to note**: related to candidate use 3 above, which is about permanent identifiers; this is about observing change, not resolving citations. *OCR*: no. *Personal-data endpoint*: no. *Cost*: within free tiers [ASSUMPTION].

## Evidence Against the Idea

1. **Publishing expunged proceedings may breach parliamentary privilege.** Reported verbatim: "Publishing expunged proceedings violates parliamentary privilege." This lands directly on candidate use #2 — the most novel gap found is also the most legally exposed, and the exposure falls on an individual, not an institution. The practical form of the risk (what is actually actionable, and whether publishing a *diff* differs from publishing the expunged text) was **not** researched. — [source: derekobrien.in] (confidence: medium on the statement, cited; **low** on legal consequence) **This contradicts the attractiveness of the single most promising novel use, and is stated here rather than buried.**
2. **The field is more crowded than "underused" suggests.** At least 8 live projects plus PRS, ADR, TCPD and data.gov.in catalogs. One cluster (SansadSaar/ParliamentWatch/IndiaVotes) already runs continuous 2-hourly ingestion with ~243K+ records on free infrastructure — materially the owner's stated end-state. The honest reading of the premise is *"the data is hard to access and the existing attempts are fragmented and little-known"*, not *"nobody has done this."* — [source: github.com, 8 repos] (confidence: high)
3. **Demand may be real but small.** The best quantified signals are 549 downloads for a current-MP dump and 35 for a committee-report corpus. Neither suggests a large audience. — [source: zenodo.org] (confidence: medium — download counts are a weak proxy and these are two unlike datasets)
4. **The citizen audience is unevidenced.** Every located demand signal is from a researcher, developer, think tank or politician. Two of the three named audiences in the intake are supported; "citizens" is not, yet. (confidence: medium — absence of found evidence)
5. **Parliament itself is moving up the stack.** A Digital Sansad app with AI transcription exists. If the Secretariat ships searchable transcripts and structured records, a third-party format-repair layer loses its reason to exist. The scope of this official capability is **UNVERIFIED** and is the biggest single uncertainty in this document. (confidence: low, but high impact) Update 2026-10-08: the owner's browser observation shows per-volume debate PDF downloads exist and the search box returns results for a free-text query. The earlier wording "metadata filters only, no public export path" is withdrawn. Whether the search matches inside the debate text, and what KEYWORD COUNT means, remain UNVERIFIED.
6. **The 2h/week budget collides with the dominant failure mode.** Every access route is undocumented or irregular: a route found in a JS bundle, a 404 where a sibling endpoint works, 500/400 on committee routes, no sitemap, two canonical hosts already dead, PDL search needing two different scrapers, PDFs with random suffixes.
